# Extended RCAS strength

Cort prefers stronger RCAS to the previous RCAS-plus-NIS stack. The J2 Sharpness slider remains 0..200, with 0 off and the default at 100. Up to 100 uses the existing RCAS math. Above 100, the same shader progressively raises its global negative-lobe magnitude ceiling from 3/16 to 7/32. The contrast-dependent clipping limiters, noise attenuation, alpha passthrough and existing HDR compression/expansion remain. No NIS dispatch or compilation is initiated by this live slider; its standalone helper/tests remain available.

This is NeuralAlias's extension of AMD's RCAS, not an upstream AMD mode. [AMD's source](https://github.com/GPUOpen-Effects/FidelityFX-FSR/blob/master/ffx-fsr/ffx_fsr1.h) defines the standard 3/16 ceiling and the normalization denominator `1 + 4*lobe`. Keeping the extended ceiling below 1/4 makes that denominator at least 1/8. Multiplying the original strength beyond one without adjusting the bounds could instead cross zero. Existing 0..100 settings retain their mapping; saved 101..200 settings now select extended RCAS. Higher strengths can emphasize edge outlines/noise; no universal halo-free claim is made.

The numerical GPU fixture exercises 100, 101, 125, 150, 175, 200 and out-of-range values, verifies continuous onset and increasing soft-edge contrast, preserves flat regions/HDR/variable alpha, and checks finite nonnegative RGB. On the RTX 5070 Ti the measured soft-edge contrast rises from 0.486816 at 100 to 0.502930 at 200. The live J2 fixture also runs 200 through recording/submission and checks that sharpening was applied without a NIS frame. This fixture uses a fake NGX model, not a game-quality comparison.
