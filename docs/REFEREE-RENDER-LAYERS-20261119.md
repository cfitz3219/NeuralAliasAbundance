# Following the displayed scene when render targets swap roles

## What the Shadow of War run establishes

Cort reported stronger reflection flicker in build 2e062ef. The latest results on disk were the November 18 23:38 local collection, PID 33800. Aligning the screen in PR 169 confirmed DLAA at the displayed scene in 173 of 180 saved evaluations. The six captures contained 97 even and 83 odd ordinals. In every one, the submitted scene color had incarnation 3, the depth had incarnation 2, and the captured input and the closed presented frame had the same ordinal. Every saved evaluation succeeded. The native jitter log and capture both take their ordinal from the provider's frame counter before Present advances it.

In Fable's independent measurement, odd frames in each captured group arrived at DLAA with 4.6–5.4% lower mean brightness. The change concentrates at glows and rims, and 44–55% of its magnitude survives reconstruction on affected pixels. Both presets show it. The older build also showed it with the depth correction enabled and disabled. A related portion of displayed variation is predicted by the original scene color after accounting for reconstructed sharpening. Fixing a selected depth read cannot by itself reconcile two differently lit color inputs. There is no matched native-only comparison here that assigns the total visible worsening to one change.

The draw log supplies a mechanism worth qualifying:

- In 14 complete logged draw sequences there are two same-size floating-point scene targets. Their ordered reads are A B A B B B A A, with A and B representing the first and second distinct physical targets read **in that frame**. The physical targets exchange jobs. Taking the first four sequences to identify the rule, the first read predicts the pair's final read in all ten remaining sequences. Always choosing one physical target can do at best six of ten. This checks predictability of a resource role. It does not show which draw produced a reflection or make the final read a safe injection point.
- In a separate, longer stretch, completed-frame reports 11196–11455 alternate for 260 consecutive records between choosing the later candidate and holding the earlier one. Both were written and subsequently read. In 5ebf86a the same pattern lasted 238 records, 41332–41569. The diagnostic counter stops after 300 scene reports. The six raw menu captures occur later, so the resource-role trace and the brightness measurements are **independent observations from the run**, not per-draw attribution over the same frames.
- The jitter counters are sampled at DLAA evaluation. They count how much work has happened *up to that point*. More draws at that instant can mean different game work, insertion at different stages, or both. They do not establish the game added 190 glow draws to an entire frame.
- Fable corrected the K/M comparison using identical tiles for input and output. Actual jitter follows the reported direction and scale. In the matched-tile comparison, K's whole-image departure was about 0.28 px on one axis versus 0.08 px on M. The previously quoted multi-pixel K drift was a measurement error.

## Exact gaps and the smallest runtime change

1. `native11/frame_model.hpp` already identifies lighting after material buffers and gathers candidate read/write positions. Among eligible same-depth candidates, `AfterLayers` favors draw count. `Choose` requires the same new physical color for three successive frames. An alternating role can therefore be visible in its own notes indefinitely while the selected resource stays fixed. Removing this guard globally would make noise drive selection.
2. `native11/provider.cpp::CheckScreenReader` challenges a direct pixel-shader read of the selected scene. It requires an immediate context, one nearly full-size target, one viewport, and a qualified base subresource. `Dispatch_d` observes depth correction, while the copy and resolve handlers track reads and selected-target writes. Those paths do not prove a different color branch reaches the reflected pixels. The broader frame model already summarizes deferred lists. Reuse those events as candidates; an observation must never reinterpret an unknown or inaccessible operation as a safe insertion site.
3. `core/referee_selection.hpp::Preview` can propose motion/jitter checks, delivery continuity or a different boundary. Its positive delivery vote has no separate test for one locally unstable layer. A plausible scene source can therefore receive a correct delivery vote at both an early and a late stage. Preserve that vote and open a local quality investigation independently.
4. `native11/provider.cpp` increments scene generation for a new physical color. In `native_d3d11/live_dlaa.cpp` this can reset flow and DLAA history. Treating two alternating textures as simple source switches would repeatedly throw away the very history the effect needs. Keep a stable *logical* scene generation across verified equivalent members. Preserve real resets for cuts, other views, resolution or subresource changes, and incompatible inputs. Synchronize and track the lifetime of the actual bound resource every frame.

**First runtime repair to qualify:** Retain the existing, working route. When the same two candidates recur, use the observed producer/consumer prefix to nominate a member each frame. A frame number, texture format or draw count alone cannot make the choice. Reuse `ScreenCheck` to test each candidate's contribution separately and the registered Referee comparison for what actually reached the display. On the conflicting path, compare the original color, the image handed back after sharpening, and the affected displayed region over *consecutive* frames for both rendering orders. An isolated additive glow may need correction at its join while the base scene stays on its proven route. An alternate route must preserve compatible depth, motion and jitter and genuine light changes. Keep the existing route on a new, ambiguous or contradicted order. Stop exploring once settled and reopen on contradictory evidence. No continuous all-draw capture, blocking readback or game-name rule is needed.

The existing native Results summary now recognizes long unbroken alternation in historical logs. Missing, concurrent or ambiguous candidate reads give no finding. This adds no runtime recording or GPU work. There is no deployed lighting correction in this change.

## Implemented native DX11 route

The Referee can now follow the lighting role described above. The existing layered-scene detector supplies the candidate set. On a completed frame there must be exactly two compatible floating-point targets at the selected depth and size. One has to be read after its final draw and before the other's final scene draw. The second must subsequently be read. The learner sees at least three examples of each physical owner and learns how the first actual writer predicts the later owner. This works when an owner repeats. It also works in renderers whose later owner writes first. A change in this relationship restarts learning.

The predicted owner must have received its current scene write. The actual bound base view, scene depth, viewport and reader are checked before evaluation. A clear, copy or resolve over the color after that draw, or a later depth clear, invalidates the observed version. Each owner and its actual consumer must independently reproduce the eight-sample GPU marker on the presented picture. The original route continues during this search. Three consecutive sightings and proofs for both members permit takeover. A missing or contradicted reader releases the reservation for the next frame. A completely new order can therefore have a transition miss. Learning uses the existing draw records. GPU confirmation is asynchronous.

The evaluated texture and its lifetime ID remain physical. The history key uses the qualified role generation and physical depth. A new depth, an incompatible view, a recreated member, a lost route, a real cut or a gap still gives the evaluator its usual reset signals. A physical color swap alone leaves both DLAA and measured motion history intact. The Results CMD reports successful deliveries on the role separately from observations of candidates.

**Check against saved gameplay.** In all 14 available complete two-target sequences from PID 33800, the physical owner opposite the first writer received the last scene draw and the last sampled read. The required earlier read fell before the last scene draw each time. Both owners appeared. The draw records and the six later image captures were made at different times. They cannot establish which version of the lit buffer was on screen at an individual captured reflection.

**RTX picture test.** The native fixture renders a stationary reflective highlight from interpolated surface coordinates over lit geometry. It exchanges two textures in an irregular seven-frame pattern and includes a same-shader reader whose output is discarded. It exercises both a packed HDR path and typeless FP16 with typed views, a separate compositor, and an odd number of padding rows. The fixture runs NVIDIA DLAA, reads the actual displayed reflection, and compares it with the separately saved input and reconstruction. A fixed-owner control measures the remaining native reconstruction variation, and extinguishing the highlight checks that genuine light changes remain visible. A separate clear between the lighting pass and the reader tests invalidation.

The first exploratory fixture used a screen-locked high-frequency stripe generated from pixel addresses while reporting projection jitter. Correct ownership reduced its pixel changes from 31.79 to 7.14 red levels per frame. That fixture mixed source routing with inconsistent sampling. The surface-coordinate fixture addresses the routing question directly. High-frequency shimmer from mismatched sampling remains a separate possible artifact.

The new route qualifies visible DX11 pixel-shader readers. A compute-only compositor, an ambiguous set of three HDR candidates, or an unobserved view will continue through the existing route. The repair has no game-name, preset, physical-address or even/odd frame rule. Live Shadow of War confirmation belongs to Cort. Fable's separate investigation into swaying and motion remains open.
