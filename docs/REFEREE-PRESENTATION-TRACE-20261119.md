# Accepted presentation and regional flicker evidence

The Referee now joins three images from each frame in a pair: the color actually submitted to DLAA, its output, and the back buffer just before Present. The copies run on the verified submission queue and retire by fence. A receipt for the same swap chain and ordinal must report an accepted full Present before either native reader challenge or the new regional comparison can vote. Test, rejected, occluded and partial Present calls cannot supply that proof. An accepted flip-model Present is evidence of submission to DXGI. The Desktop Window Manager and the display can still present or discard queued frames later.

The extra preceding output and screen copies run at most once every 750 ms in production. There is no GPU or CPU wait attached to the game callback. The comparison uses the existing 128×72 GPU summaries. It checks adjacent frames of a continuous verified scene, filters out major changes, and looks for a regional brightness transition while most regions remain quiet. Two pairs must show a comparable reversal in the same neighbourhood. The Results script reports the earliest measured stage and the region, frame pair, and input and proof identities. Native off-by-a-few-row centered registration is eligible after the existing independent registration vote. A rescaled or geometrically unregistered comparison can still provide ordinary delivery measurements.

Each eligible replacement still goes through the existing short coded-pixel challenge before it can steer. The DX11 path restores the marked source after the reader. The DX12 challenge associates its sample with the submitted recording, queue, presentation ordinal and return status. A rejection cannot approve the replacement. For the periodic-light case, the relevant evidence is multiple independent regions in accepted frames, so a normal every-other-frame image cannot be mistaken for the challenge. Existing ordinary-path fallback and per-reader retirement apply. The new stage finding is diagnostic evidence. Its localized brightness comparison alone offers no alternate route and cannot authorize a settings change or a whole-route rollback.

## Controlled replacement

Native DX11 can nominate the fully lit reader when two physical render targets exchange roles. The provider must observe both roles, see each reader's marker arrive in the accepted presentation and close three complete scene frames before offering the experiment. That logic uses draw and read order, view identity, and presented pixels. It uses the same rules for all games.

The shared verdict starts by measuring the game's picture on the incumbent route. It requires two brightening and two darkening transitions in a consistent region. It then measures the proposed route and briefly restores the incumbent to see whether the original problem returns. All three arms require comparable views, extents, registration and rendering settings. It skips the first 16 frames after each switch. It keeps an alternative only when a verified DLAA result is still reaching the presentation and the maximum observed regional change falls by at least 30% and 0.04 stops against both incumbent windows. The second incumbent window must reproduce the original amplitude within the allowed band. An uncertain, timed-out or worse result restores ordinary delivery. A matched scene can retry quiet baseline measurements. An unsuccessful alternative stays rejected for the current render-route identity.

The brightness check retains 16 signed measurements in each region so neighboring brightening and darkening cannot cancel. It checks that the same local pattern returns in the final incumbent measurement and bounds any shift in the region's average brightness. On the six saved Shadow of War sequences from PID 33800, an offline check of the same thumbnail measurement found three sequences with four agreeing adjacent-pair transitions after screening for registration, edge correlation and quiet surroundings. Those sequences were 23:36:59, 23:37:13 and 23:37:27. Their four-transition strengths were 0.270, 0.399 and 0.399 stops. The other three did not provide four comparable adjacent pairs. The saved run contains only incumbent pictures.

An adjacent back-buffer pair may still show flicker when the incumbent changes its input texture or resets DLAA history. The trial compares those accepted pictures within the same arm. Motion scoring and attribution of a change to a specific render stage still require continuity of the source. The existing GPU sampler reuses its bounded scratch allocations and permits trial pairs at most every 400 ms. Results reports each verdict with the two incumbent measurements, the replacement measurement and the reason. No user capture is required.

## What the previous failures teach

- Shadow of War showed a repeating difference in the input and a separate false-motion effect in the output. Fable's round 13 replay held the recorded sequence steady and changed one input at a time. Removing background motion in same-parity replays reduced the measured slow field by 67–86 percent. Reaching the screen and having correct motion are independent questions.
- Shadow of War's scene arrived at 3440×1433 inside a 3440×1440 presentation. Registering the actual pixels changed the conclusion. Equal screen sizes or a visually close image are weak evidence for ownership of the final pass.
- Sniper's deferred submissions and late descriptors made recording-time guesses unreliable. Store the executed writer, actual resource view, context version and the queue handoff before drawing conclusions from a read. A queue-relative fence orders capture and measurement on supported paths.
- A full-frame average hides small reflective and HUD regions. Separate regions give a better way to probe known failure classes. A stable foreground plus an oscillating sky can leave whole-screen delivery and motion judgements positive.
- Scene changes, view-size changes, frame gaps and ordinary native illumination can all resemble flicker. Correlate independent regional evidence and preserve the established renderer when the evidence is ambiguous.

## Scope of the first implementation

This traces image stages and independently preflights observed readers. The native D3D11 and D3D12 Present/Present1 hooks supply exact acceptance. Streamline boundaries, frame-generated proxy frames, and obscure copies without a verified queue and ordinal still need their own accepted-frame link before they can provide this proof. The shared comparison can be used by any provider that supplies registered presentation evidence and can safely activate and restore a verified alternative. Native DX11's rotating lighting reader is the first such adapter. The regional metric covers repeatable local luminance changes. Fine-edge shimmer, geometric motion distortion, and scan-out cadence require different evidence. A successful comparison qualifies the observed rendering route, and game testing covers how it behaves across other gameplay.

### Which observation should change which decision

| Observation from past games | Appropriate response and present scope |
|---|---|
| DLAA stops at a bow, camera cut or menu transition | Check eligible frames and the last executed writer/consumer order. Retain ordinary delivery while a returning reader earns proof. Examine temporal inputs separately if submission continues. |
| A glare layer, HUD or reflection differs locally while most of the result reaches the screen | Inspect the first changing stage, then compare only a qualified reversible alternative. A whole-screen success cannot clear a local fault. Current trials act on repeatable local brightness changes in comparable views. |
| The far background moves with a near character while input pixels remain still | Check the motion field and the subsequent reconstruction. Fable owns the supported near-actor contamination finding. Changing a reader or invalidating all history cannot establish a better vector field. |
| Render scale, viewport, projection, or HDR changes | Re-establish matching frame, extents, settings and geometry before carrying a decision forward. A differently sized resource or a physically recycled texture can represent a different logical image. |
| Two sources are visually identical, a composition pass is opaque, or the frame is generated by a proxy | Preserve existing delivery while independent evidence is unavailable. Native DX12 has screen preflight and stage tracing. A reversible actuator for the shared quality trial on DX12, exact identity for generated presentations, and counterfactual motion-field tests remain separate engineering capabilities. |

## Following up an unsuccessful placement

The native DX11 and DX12 renderers now pass the earliest measured change and the controls actually recorded on both accepted frames to a common recovery controller. It keeps up to eight combinations of scene, settings, view, region and local flash pattern in memory. Returning to a matching view retains its exclusions during that session. A resize, device change or Referee opt-out clears the relevant history. No setting is written to the user's configuration by a trial.

| First observed change | Controls eligible for a temporary comparison |
|---|---|
| DLAA input | Halve the existing Bloom contribution. If eligible, compare current-frame XeGTAO against its accumulated result. |
| DLAA output | Keep camera motion and omit its optional object-flow correction, or use measured motion without the fitted fill on camera-free frames. Each requires a matching submitted motion supplier and measurable correspondence. |
| After DLAA, on the accepted presentation | Compare half-strength RCAS when the sharpening pass actually ran. An opaque game compositor can leave this stage with no remaining eligible control. |

The DX11 lighting-placement comparison gets the first opportunity when its reader is verified. On a failure, ordinary rendering resumes and the observer locates the fault again across input, reconstruction and presentation. A confirmed failure is specific to the measured view and control. Loss of evidence defers that control, giving another eligible control priority. Each arm has a bounded observation window, renewed only on a phase transition. Quiet frames and slow collection do not renew it.

The GPU worker compares the same visible area across original, candidate and original settings. Gains must survive restoration, loss of detail in the region disqualifies a candidate, and moving the flash elsewhere fails the comparison. A single exceptional frame cannot rule a tool out. Screen motion trials also check the declared motion against image correspondences. A retained control is sampled periodically and withdrawn if delivery is lost or the artifact recurs. The Results report distinguishes a measured rejection from a view change, an incomplete comparison and exhaustion of the available tools. Game-owned lighting passes that the runtime cannot separately observe or change remain a coverage limit.
